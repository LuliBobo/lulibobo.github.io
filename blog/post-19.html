One Changed Account Number. No Way Back.

Post #18 ended on an invoice that was correct and still waiting. This one is about an invoice that is correct, approved and about to be paid to an account nobody confirmed, and about the one check where slower is better.

A regular supplier writes that its bank has changed and that the new account is on the next invoice. The invoice arrives, and everything about it is right: the amount, the order, the VAT, the delivery. It is approved, and the owner of the cost centre has confirmed it. The only new thing is the account. Here is the path.

StepWhat happenedWhat was missing
E-mail"Our bank has changed, the new account is on the invoice"Nobody called a number that was already on file
Vendor fileAccount updated in the supplier recordWho is allowed to change it, and who confirms
AI assistantRead the invoice and the new accountIt compares the invoice with itself, not with the agreement
ApprovalInvoice correct and approved (Post #18)The document was confirmed, the account was not
PaymentFull amount goes to the new accountIn many cases there is no way back

Every step worked. The check that was missing is a different kind of check from the ones above, and it would have taken one phone call.

Not the same change as in Post #16

Post #16 asked whether a payment is normal for this supplier, and the answer came from the amount. A changed account is a different kind of deviation, and it is harder to see.

The amount differs from historyThe account differs from history
How it looksLike a possible mistakeLike a normal update
Who tends to noticeA check against the supplier's normalOften nobody, because it is a change in master data
If missedA wrong amount, which can often be correctedA payment to the wrong party, which often can't be recovered

Where speed goes wrong

An efficient payment run pays the approved invoice on time. In this case that is exactly the problem. The check that matters here makes the payment slower, once, for one supplier, until someone has confirmed the change another way.

What it looks likeWhat's actually true
The invoice is from our supplier The invoice says it is. Whether the new account is theirs hasn't been confirmed yet
We have an approval process It approves the document. Nobody approved the account
The system saved the new account Saved is not confirmed
A change of bank details in an e-mail An invitation to pay, with a very trustworthy signature

Why the tools alone don't settle it

The ERP stores the new account. An AI assistant reads the invoice and the new account and writes both down correctly. Both compare the invoice with itself, so both agree. What neither can know is whether the supplier actually changed its bank. That needs a different channel, such as a call to a number you already had before the e-mail arrived, and a person who is responsible for confirming it.

I am not a lawyer or a bank. What happens after a payment to the wrong account, including recovery, liability and insurance, is a question for your bank and your lawyer, not for this post.

Post #18 applies again: a confirmed document is not yet a confirmed account. And Post #8 applies too. A warning on every invoice teaches people to stop reading it. This one should fire only on a changed account, with the reason attached.

What a bank-details check has to contain

ElementWhy it matters
A change of account is its own event, not a line on an invoiceIt doesn't get lost between the amounts
Confirmation through another channel, such as a call to the number already on fileAn e-mail is not proof of who sent it
Two people: one records the change, another confirms itOne mistake doesn't turn into a payment
The first payment to a new account is held until it is confirmedHere, waiting is the correct result
A warning for a payment to an account changed in the last few days, with the reason attachedA warning without a reason can't be judged
A person decides, and the check changes and blocks nothing by itselfThe responsibility stays with someone who can be asked

For a changed bank account, the right speed is slower than usual.

A test you can run this week

Take the last three changes of a supplier's bank account. For each one, write down who recorded it, who confirmed it and how. The number of changes where you can't answer "how" is your figure.

There is no script behind this one either. It is a short list and a rule about who confirms, and both are worth having before any tool is involved.
