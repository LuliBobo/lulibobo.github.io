The Average Looked Fine. One Product Didn't.

Post #16 showed that you can't spot an odd payment until you know the normal one. This one asks what the monthly average margin is hiding, and how a product or a customer can go below zero without anyone seeing it.

Boris Dračka · October 2026 · 6 min read

A monthly average is a very polite way of not mentioning something. Post #16 argued that you can't see an odd payment until you know the normal one. Margin has the opposite problem: the average is so normal that it covers whatever sits below it.

What stacked up on Line C

There was no single bad decision behind Line C. In many teams the negative sign arrives as several small items, each of which is explainable on its own. In this illustration the largest one is six points.

ItemEffect on Line C margin
Margin on product cost aloneplus 10 points
Discount for the largest customerminus 6 points
Returnsminus 4 points
Freightminus 5 points
Payment feesminus 2 points
Sales mix: more of the lower-priced packminus 3 points
Margin on Line Cminus 10%

The same arithmetic works per customer. In this illustration the largest customer of Line C buys 40% of the line at minus 16%, while the other customers sit around minus 6%. The line total of minus 10% hides both.

The sign depends on what you count

Margin on a product is a decision about which costs belong to it, not a fact lying in the ledger. The same Line C is plus 10% if you count only product cost, and minus 10% once discounts, returns, freight and payment fees are inside. A third version, with allocated overhead, would be lower still. None of them is "the true one". What matters is picking one basis and using it for every line, because otherwise you are comparing different things.

What it looks likeWhat's actually true
Blended margin is 19%, so everything is fine One line of three is below zero, and the average can hide it
Margin is a number Margin is a number plus a decision about which costs count
A line below zero comes from one big mistake It often builds up from several small items, none of them alarming alone
The average temperature of the building is comfortable One room is freezing and another is overheating. The report is a very good thermostat for the building and a poor one for either room

The same question as in Post #16

Post #16 asked "compared with what?" for a payment: you need the supplier's own normal. Here the question is the same, with the average in the role of the normal. Compared with the blended 19%, Line C looks like a small drag. Compared with zero, it is a loss. The check has to look at the part, not only at the whole.

Post #8 applies here too. A check that fires on every small dip teaches people to stop reading it. The threshold is a decision, and the reason attached to each alert is what makes it usable.

What a margin check has to contain

ElementWhy it matters
Margin per product line and per customer, not only in totalThe total cannot show a part below zero
One cost basis for every rowOtherwise the rows aren't comparable
Month by month, not one snapshotIt shows the drift towards zero before the line crosses it
An alert when a line crosses zero or an agreed threshold, with the reason attached (discount, freight, returns, mix)An alert without a reason is not actionable
A person confirms what happens nextThe check changes no price and stops no sale

One more thing sits underneath the table. Margin is only as current as the slowest unconfirmed document. The discount, the freight charge or the return often sits on an invoice or a credit note that nobody has confirmed yet, so the report looks complete and isn't.

The average answers "how are we doing?". The question that costs money is "which part is losing?"

A test you can run this week

Take last month. Calculate the margin on your ten largest customers or products, on one cost basis, and look at the lowest three. Did you know their sign before you calculated it?

I haven't put a script behind this one either. It is a table and a decision about the cost basis, and both are worth making before any tool is involved.
