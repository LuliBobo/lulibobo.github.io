You can't see odd without knowing normal

A single limit, say "anything above €10,000", sees the same thing in every supplier: a number. But €10,000 is routine for one supplier and enormous for another. What stands out is a payment that differs from that supplier's own history.

None of the three is damning on its own. A supplier can send a larger invoice, change banks, and ask for a Friday payment. Together they are worth a phone call to a number you already have.

Most odd payments are mistakes

What it looks likeWhat's actually true
A payment check is for catching fraud In many teams I've seen, most unusual payments turn out to be mistakes: a duplicate, a typo in an amount, a wrong date
One limit for every supplier is enough One limit is too high for small suppliers and too low for large ones. Each supplier's own history is the better yardstick
An alert should be rare An alert that fires every day is just a very expensive calendar
More sensitive is safer More sensitive means more false alarms, and Post #8 showed what comes next: people stop reading them

Too tight, too loose, about right

The tolerance is a decision, not a setting. On this composite week, a tight band flags nine payments, a loose band flags none (the odd one included), and the band in the middle flags exactly one.

An alert is only as good as the normal it is compared with.

What a check like this has to contain

PartWhat it does
A baseline per supplier The usual amount and spread, the usual weekday and the known accounts, from the last several months
A tolerance How far from usual counts as unusual, tuned on past weeks rather than guessed
Reasons Every flag says why, in plain words
A human step The check only reports. A person decides what to do

I put together a small script that does exactly this against made-up payments: it builds each supplier's baseline from the earlier weeks, checks one week against it and prints what looks different, with the reason. It only reads and reports, and it is no substitute for the controls your organisation already has. It's in the same personal, non-commercial repository as the earlier demos in this series.

The first test I'd run is a simple one: take last month's payments, sort them by how far each is from its own supplier's history, and read the top ten. Count how many are mistakes, how many are explained, and how many deserve a call.
