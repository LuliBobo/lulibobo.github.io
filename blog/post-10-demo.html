The Agent That Notices Idle Cash
Before Anyone Adds It Up

A small script that compares each account's balance to its buffer and estimates what the excess could have earned instead — before the number quietly disappears into next month's close.

Boris Dračka · August 2026 · Companion demo

This is a personal, non-commercial side project I put together to learn — not a product or service, and not connected to any company I work with.

Post #10 asked a simple question: if a balance sits above the agreed operating buffer for weeks, who actually notices — and what does that silence cost? Below is a small prototype that answers the second half of that question with sample data.

The script doesn't decide anything. It just puts the one number — what the excess could have earned — next to the balance that was already sitting there in plain sight.

The sample data

Six accounts, each with a balance, an agreed buffer, and the rate a routine cash sweep could reasonably earn. Two of them are carrying meaningfully more than their buffer calls for.

How it works

1
Load the balancesReads a CSV with one row per account: balance, buffer, and an illustrative alternative rate.
2
Compare to bufferFor each account, subtracts the buffer from the balance to get the excess sitting idle.
3
Apply a thresholdAnything above a configurable excess amount (default €50,000) gets flagged for review.
4
Estimate forgone interestMultiplies the flagged excess by the alternative rate to estimate what a sweep could have earned.
5
Print a summaryLists the flagged accounts and the estimated annual and monthly opportunity cost.

Try it yourself

1 — Clone the repository

# same personal, non-commercial repo as the earlier demos in this series git clone https://github.com/LuliBobo/cfo-agents.git cd cfo-agents

2 — Add your own balances (optional)

account_id,account_name,balance,buffer,alt_rate_pct AC-01,Operating Account,340000,150000,3.0 AC-04,Project Account,120000,40000,3.0 # ...add as many rows as you have accounts

3 — Run it

python idle_cash_agent.py data/account_balances.csv --threshold 50000 --rate 3.0

What it's built from

Data source

A plain CSV of account balances — no live banking connection, no API keys, nothing beyond sample data.

Calculation

Standard Python, no external dependencies — subtraction and a rate applied to the excess.

Configurable threshold

The flagging amount and the alternative rate are both command-line arguments, not hardcoded.

Read-only by design

The script only reads and reports. It doesn't move money, connect to a bank, or take any action on its own.
